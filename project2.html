Rolls-Royce Financial Performance Analysis

How Rolls-Royce Holdings plc recovered from the pandemic, measured through growth, margins, returns on capital and cash generation from 2020 to 2025.

Project Overview

This project analysed Rolls-Royce Holdings plc using underlying financial data from its annual reports and results announcements covering 2020 to 2025 (latest: FY2025 results, published 26 February 2026). The objective was to evaluate the company's revenue growth, profitability, operational efficiency and overall financial recovery following the COVID-19 pandemic.

An interactive Excel dashboard communicates the key performance indicators, historical trends and a five-year forecast to support strategic decision-making.

Business Questions

  • How has Rolls-Royce's revenue changed from 2020 to 2025?
  • How effectively has the company recovered since the pandemic?
  • What trends can be seen in profitability and operating performance?
  • How efficiently is capital being used?
  • What do forecasts suggest about future growth?
  • How has cash generation improved over time?

Key Findings

Finding 01

Strong post-pandemic recovery

Revenue rose from £10.9bn in 2021 to £20.1bn in 2025, a compound annual growth rate of about 16%, with double-digit growth in every year from 2022.

Finding 02

Margins transformed

The operating margin moved from -17.6% in 2020 to 17.3% in 2025. Operating profit grew from £0.4bn in 2021 to £3.46bn, including 40.5% growth in 2025 alone.

Finding 03

Returns on capital improved every year

Return on capital rose from 11.3% in 2023 to 13.8% in 2024 and 18.9% in 2025, showing that profit growth outpaced the capital invested in the business.

Finding 04

Net debt turned into net cash

Free cash flow swung from -£4.3bn in 2020 to £3.3bn in 2025, with cash conversion rising from 78% (2022) to 94% (2025). This took the balance sheet from £5.2bn of net debt in 2021 to £1.9bn of net cash at the end of 2025.

Forecast & Outlook

Base-Case Assumptions

My base case assumes 7% annual revenue growth from 2026 to 2030, with the operating margin rising from 18.0% to 19.0%. This gives operating profit of £3.86bn in 2026 and £4.67bn in 2028.

This is deliberately more conservative than management's guidance of £4.0–4.2bn for 2026 and £4.9–5.2bn by 2028. My margin assumption sits within the guided 18–20% range, so the gap comes from slower revenue growth. That reflects the main risks: a slowdown in aerospace demand, supply-chain disruption, trade tariffs and changes to defence budgets.
